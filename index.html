04 / GROWING WISDOM

Read the field, not just the calendar

A reliable grower watches several systems at once. Here is what changes your plants’ chances.

☼

Sun & day length

Sunlight powers photosynthesis. Most fruiting crops need 6–8+ hours of direct sun; leafy crops can often manage less. Day length shifts with latitude and season, affecting flowering and crop development.

ESTABLISHED SCIENCE
♧

Soil & water

Check soil temperature before sowing. Start with a soil test, add organic matter when appropriate, mulch to moderate moisture and temperature, and water at the root zone instead of on a rigid schedule.

ESTABLISHED SCIENCE
❄

Frost & microclimates

Average frost dates describe risk, not a promise. Cold pockets, elevation, wind, slopes, and buildings all matter. Harden off seedlings and monitor forecasts near the edges of your season.

ESTABLISHED SCIENCE
◕

The Moon & traditions

Lunar phases are real and predictable. Planting above-ground crops while waxing and roots while waning is a traditional practice, but reliable evidence that moon phase improves yields is lacking. Garden by conditions first.

TRADITION, NOT VALIDATED
✿

Living ecosystems

Observe pests before treating. Encourage pollinators and natural enemies, rotate plant families where practical, remove diseased material, and use integrated pest management rather than routine sprays.

ESTABLISHED PRACTICE
↺

Observe & adapt

Record varieties, sowing dates, soil conditions, and harvests. A local journal improves your planting estimates across years, because no national calendar knows every patch of ground.

GROWER'S METHOD
◐

Lunar planting, without pretending folklore is physics

—

Use the moon cycle as an optional observation ritual, never as a reason to miss a safe soil- or weather-based planting window.
