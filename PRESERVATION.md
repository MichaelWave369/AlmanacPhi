# AlmanacΦ · Harvest, Preserve & Save

This module is an **educational field guide**, not a tested canning recipe database. A recipe-specific, current USDA/NCHFP or cooperative extension publication is required to determine **whether** a food can be safely canned and exactly **how**. No generalized processing times, pressures, or acid ratios are generated here.

## Choose a safe pathway

| Method | Typical use | Safety boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Boiling-water canning | Foods that meet a **specific tested** high-acid or acidified product process | No plain low-acid vegetables; require tested recipe, jar/pack parameters and altitude adjustment. |
| Pressure canning | Certain low-acid foods when a tested procedure exists | Ordinary boiling water is not safe for these foods. Verify actual pressure canner, gauge, altitude and time. |
| Freezing | Most vegetables and many herbs | Blanch crops when advised; freezing does **not** sterilize produce. |
| Dehydrating | Selected fruits, herbs and vegetables | Use food-specific controlled drying guidance; protect from moisture and mold. |
| Fermentation | Tested sauerkraut and fermented vegetables | Use a tested salt/temperature/monitoring recipe; a ferment is not automatically shelf-stable. |
| Refrigerator pickles | Pickled produce kept chilled | Not shelf-stable, regardless of jar type; follow a tested recipe and storage period. |
| Cool & cold storage | Chilled perishables and suitable cured crops | Storage needs are crop-specific. Refrigerator at or below 40°F (4°C). |
| Seed saving | Garden propagation | Start with open-pollinated crops; isolate cross-pollinating varieties and store dry/cool. |

### Canning: non-negotiable decisions

1. Use an **exact** tested recipe for your product. Safe steps for one preparation (plain, pickled, raw-packed, hot-packed, whole, crushed, pureed, mixed) are not interchangeable.
2. Use the jar size, ingredients, headspace, canner and process prescribed. Check equipment and the **elevation where the canner is operated**, not just a ZIP code's regional average.
3. Tomatoes require specified acidification for tested methods that call for it, including certain pressure-canner options. See [NCHFP acidification instructions](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/can/how-do-i-can-tomatoes/tomato-acidification-directions/).
4. Plain low-acid vegetables (beans, corn, carrots and other vegetables) need tested pressure-canner recipes if canned. For summer squash, NCHFP does not recommend improvising a plain home-canning recipe.
5. Never treat a seal, smell, or taste as proof of safety. If a jar's integrity or processing is suspect, do not sample it. Follow official disposal guidance.
6. Never invent a canning schedule or substitute ingredients in a tested process without explicit permission from that source. A personal pH reading does not substitute for laboratory process validation.
7. The AlmanacΦ pantry log is just a **record**: an HTTPS recipe reference is required to log a canning batch, but recording a URL does not verify the recipe, the process or the food's safety.

### Freezing

Most vegetables need crop-specific blanching. Blanching durations vary by vegetable and size. Cool promptly, pack in suitable containers, remove excess air and freeze at **0°F (-18°C) or below**. Freezer storage durations are primarily about **quality** when food remains continuously frozen, not a guarantee about handling before freezing. See the [NCHFP blanching reference](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/freeze/freeze-general-information/blanching-times/).

### Drying

Clean and prepare food with a validated recipe. Use controlled drying temperatures where recommended. Cool foods fully before airtight storage. Properly dried **vegetables should generally be crisp/brittle**, and dried **fruit may need conditioning**. Recheck stored containers for moisture; discard moldy contents. See [NCHFP packaging and storing dried foods](https://nchfp.uga.edu/how/dry/general-information-drying/packaging-and-storing-dried-foods/).

### Fermenting & refrigerator pickles

Fermentation relies on precise conditions including salt and temperature. Use tested guidelines and keep finished ferments refrigerated when directed. A refrigerator pickle must remain refrigerated at **40°F (4°C) or below** and is **not** equivalent to a shelf-stable canned pickle. See [University of Minnesota fermentation guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/som/node/172346).

### Saving seed

Open-pollinated seed generally reproduces more consistently than F1 hybrid seed. Beans, peas, peppers and tomatoes often make approachable first projects. Mature seed must be properly cleaned, dried and labeled; keep it cool and dry. Cross-pollinating and biennial crops need additional planning. See [University of Minnesota seed-saving guidance](https://extension.umn.edu/garden-and-home/yard-and-garden/gardening-in-minnesota/saving-vegetable-seeds).

## Privacy and inventory

- The pantry log resides in browser `localStorage` under `almanacphi.pantry.v1`; no user accounts, sync or telemetry.
- The log records crop, method, preparation date, quantity, storage location, notes and optional recipe URL.
- An HTTPS recipe-source field is mandatory for canning **records**. It is not a verification engine.
- Export the pantry as JSON regularly, especially before changing browsers/devices or clearing site data. The existing growing log has a separate JSON export.
- This release does not generate use-by dates, classify canned batches as safe, or guarantee food quality or seed germination.
